# Pottery: Field Guide

**Age:** 4+
**Duration:** 2-4 weeks
**Location:** Chamberí + river/outdoor + pottery studio
**Artifact:** Vessel that holds water for 24 hours

---

## The Setup

You are people who live far from water. Every day someone has to walk to the river and back. Cupped hands lose most of it. Leaves tear. Gourds crack. You need something that holds water, carries it home, and keeps it.

This is not a craft project. This is a survival problem.

---

## Session 1: The Hunt for Magic Mud

**Go outside after rain.**

Collect samples. Bring containers. Dig in different spots:
- Garden soil
- Construction site earth
- Near the river
- Anywhere that looks different

Back home, test each sample:
- Can you roll it into a ball?
- Does the ball hold its shape or crumble?
- Can you poke it and have the hole stay?
- Does it stick to itself when you press two pieces together?

Most samples will fail. One or two might work.

*What makes them different?*

Don't explain. Just notice together.

**Output:** A sample that holds shape. Mark where you found it.

---

## Session 2: The Water Carrier Problem

**Make something to hold water.**

Use your good mud. Form a bowl, a cup, anything. Take your time.

Let it dry. This takes 1-2 days. Put it somewhere warm, not in direct sun (cracks).

When it's dry, it feels solid. It looks done.

Fill it with water.

Watch.

*What happens over the next hour?*

It will soften. It will leak. Eventually it will dissolve back into mud.

This is the problem. Drying isn't enough.

**Output:** Frustration. A failed bowl. The question: *now what?*

---

## Session 3: The Accident

**You need fire for this one. Campfire, chimenea, fire pit, or arrange with the pottery studio for a pit firing demo.**

Tell the story:

> "A long time ago, someone left their clay bowl too close to the fire overnight. In the morning, something strange had happened..."

Put a dried clay piece at the edge of the fire. Not in the flames—near them, where it gets very hot. Leave it for 30-60 minutes.

Let it cool completely. This is important—hot ceramics shatter if cooled too fast.

Now test it with water.

*What happened? Why doesn't it dissolve?*

The fire changed it permanently. You can't un-fire clay. This is chemistry, but you don't need to call it that.

**Output:** A fired piece. The eureka.

---

## Session 4: The Mistake

**Optional but valuable. Requires fire.**

What if you skip drying? What if you put wet clay directly in the fire?

Try it. Stand back.

*Pop. Crack. Sometimes a small explosion.*

Why? There's water trapped inside. Fire turns water to steam. Steam expands. The clay can't stretch, so it breaks.

This is why patience matters. The ancients learned this the hard way, many times.

**Output:** Broken pieces. Understanding of *why* we dry first.

---

## Session 5: The Oven

**Build a simple heat trap.**

Stack bricks or rocks around your fire pit. Leave a small opening for air.

Notice: it gets hotter inside the enclosure than outside.

This is a kiln. The first kilns were just pits with clay pieces buried in coals, covered with more fuel and earth.

The pottery studio has a kiln that gets much hotter. What they're doing is the same thing, just more controlled.

**Output:** Understanding that fire needs to be trapped to work harder.

---

## Session 6: Three Bowls

**Make three vessels:**

1. One thick (finger-width walls)
2. One very thin (as thin as you can manage)
3. One in between

Dry them all. Fire them all.

*Which one survives? Which cracks? Which is too fragile?*

There's a sweet spot. The thick one might crack because it dries unevenly. The thin one might break because it's too delicate. The middle one might work.

Or not. Try again.

**Output:** A vessel that holds water for 24 hours. Or a log of what failed and why.

---

## The Artifact

A fired vessel that holds water for 24 hours.

Plus a log:
- Where did you find your clay?
- What failed?
- What did the fire do?
- What did you change?

This is the trophy. The child made a thing that solves a problem humans had for 20,000 years.

---

## Notes for the Guide

**Don't explain too early.** Let the failures happen. Let the child ask why. If they don't ask, wait.

**You don't need to know the answers.** "I don't know—let's find out" is the right response. This is discovery for you too.

**The pottery studio** is for Session 3-6 if you don't have fire access. Ask if they do pit firing or raku. Explain what you're trying to do—most potters will be excited.

**The river** is for Session 1. The good clay is often near water, in banks where the fine particles have settled.

**Rain week** is perfect. Mud is everywhere. Test it all.

**Time pressure:** None. This can take three days or three months. The artifact is done when it holds water. Not before.
